Reddit Virality & Controversy Analysis

Team of 3 · DSAN 6000 · Spring 2026
Guiding question

What makes a Reddit post go viral: positivity, negativity or controversy?

  • Analyzed 446 GB of Reddit data (~500M posts, ~3B comments over 14 months) with PySpark on a 4-node Spark cluster on AWS EC2, reading partitioned Parquet files from S3.
  • Found a winner-take-all pattern: posts scoring 1,000+ were 0.34% of submissions but earned ~44% of all score; the viral rate rose from 1.0% at 6 UTC to 1.8% at 12–18 UTC.
  • Built a Spark NLP sentiment pipeline and TF-IDF analysis in MLlib: negative titles went viral more often than positive ones (1.50% vs. 1.23%).
  • Trained MLlib logistic regression, random forest and GBT models; random forest reached ROC-AUC 0.73 for virality on 509K posts, judged by AUC because 98.6% accuracy only reflected the ~1–2% viral rate.
  • Showed linear regression's R² for comment counts dropped from 0.20 on one month to 0.01 on all 14 months, while random forest held 0.11.

Dental Cavity Detection with Transfer Learning

Computer vision · YOLOv8
  • Collected and hand-labeled 6,000 panoramic dental X-rays in Label Studio across 4 ICCMS severity classes to build a custom object-detection dataset.
  • Fine-tuned pretrained YOLOv8 models in Python (transfer learning); compared YOLOv8n and YOLOv8s at 50 and 100 epochs, and YOLOv8s gave the best validation mAP@50 (0.70+).
